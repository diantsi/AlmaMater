# AlmaMater Critical Analysis

## Clarification Questions

| Question | Team Clarification |
|---|---|
| Is AlmaMater intended for one university or multiple universities? | The initial platform is for the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy. |
| Who may publish listings and contact students? | Any user authenticated through the university's corporate email. |
| Which features belong to the first release? | Every feature described in the current README. |

## Critical Analysis

| Risk / Weakness | Explanation | Suggested Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 — Scope is unrealistic for a student project** | The first release includes profiles, search, events, team formation, applications, chat, comments, reviews, recommendations, notifications, a map, check-ins, calendar integration, and authentication. Each represents a substantial product area. Attempting all of them risks producing many incomplete features without validating the central idea. | Define an MVP around verified profiles, structured listings, search, and RSVP/join requests. Treat chat, reviews, recommendations, map/check-ins, and calendar integration as later phases. |
| **R2 — The central problem is not validated** | The README states that information is scattered and existing channels are ineffective, but provides no interviews, survey results, or behavioral evidence from Kyiv-Mohyla Academy. The platform may address a plausible problem without addressing a sufficiently important one. | Validate the problem with students and organizers. Record which channels they use, what they miss, how often the problem occurs, and whether they would adopt another platform. |
| **R3 — No primary user or initial use case is selected** | AlmaMater serves opportunity seekers, project initiators, organizations, and staff simultaneously. Their workflows and priorities differ, making product decisions and evaluation difficult. | Select one initial interaction, such as student organizations publishing opportunities for students to discover and join. Expand into peer team formation after establishing regular usage. |
| **R4 — The platform depends on content before it has users** | Students receive little value without current listings, while organizers receive little value without an active audience. Empty or outdated listings could quickly undermine trust. | Establish a launch strategy with several university organizations or departments committed to publishing and maintaining opportunities before student onboarding. |
| **R5 — Verification is treated as trust** | A corporate email confirms university affiliation but does not establish that a listing is official, a claimed skill is genuine, or a user will behave appropriately. Allowing every authenticated user to publish and contact others creates spam and impersonation risks. | Distinguish verified university membership from verified organization/staff status. Define reporting, moderation, content ownership, and organizer verification rules. |
| **R6 — Privacy and safety boundaries are undefined** | Searchable skills, faculty details, direct invitations, reviews, map notes, and check-ins expose personal or location-related information. The README does not specify visibility controls or whether check-ins are live. | Make sensitive profile fields optional, provide visibility and contact controls, and avoid public live check-ins. Define blocking, reporting, and data-retention expectations. |
| **R7 — Reviews and endorsements may be unreliable or harmful** | The purpose and eligibility rules for reviews are unclear. Unverified endorsements can become popularity signals, while negative reviews may create disputes or reputational harm. | Remove reviews from the first release or restrict them to confirmed collaborations using structured, private feedback. Clearly define moderation and appeal rules before introducing public reputation features. |
| **R8 — Joining workflows are inconsistent** | “RSVP,” “join request,” “apply,” and “one-click registration” appear across the README without defining when each applies or who approves participation. Events, selective projects, and recurring groups require different rules. | Define explicit listing modes: open RSVP, approval-required application, and external registration. Specify capacity, waitlist, cancellation, and acceptance behavior for each. |
| **R9 — Personalization assumptions are unsupported** | Recommendations promise relevant opportunities, but a new single-university platform may initially have too little content and interaction data for meaningful personalization. | Begin with user-selected interests, filters, saved searches, or category subscriptions. Describe algorithmic recommendations as a later possibility rather than an initial value proposition. |
| **R10 — Success is not defined** | The concept lists features and benefits but does not state what result would demonstrate that AlmaMater works. Registrations alone would not prove that students discover useful opportunities or form effective teams. | Define measurable outcomes such as active listings, listing-to-join conversion, successful team matches, repeat organizer usage, and participant attendance. |
| **R11 — Adoption over existing tools is assumed** | Organizers already use Instagram, Telegram, Google Forms, email, and spreadsheets. AlmaMater adds value only if maintaining another channel is easier or produces meaningfully better reach. | Define a clear adoption advantage and minimize duplicate work—for example, reusable listings, centralized applications, exportable participant lists, or links that organizers can share through existing channels. |
