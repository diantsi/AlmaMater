# Problem
Students often struggle to discover relevant university events, projects, and opportunities because information is scattered across different platforms. Many students also have promising ideas but lack teammates with the right skills to bring them to life. Student organizations face similar challenges when trying to reach interested participants and build teams. As a result, useful ideas and opportunities are often missed. 

# Solution
The project aims to create one platform where students can discover activities, publish ideas, find teammates, and join projects or events.

# Users 
The platform is designed for:
• Students looking for opportunities
• Students looking for teammates
• Students looking to gain experience
• Student organizations and initiative groups
• Event and project organizers
• Teachers and university staff

# Feature plan
## 1. Student Profile

Each student builds a profile with:

- Name, photo, short bio
- Faculty, major, and year of study
- Areas of interest (research, volunteering, sports, arts, entrepreneurship, etc.)
- Skills and languages
- Reviews/endorsements from other students and organizers

Ideally would be to use university corporate email.

## 2. Find Events & Teammates

A search tool to find relevant activities and people with similar interests:

- Filter by category, date, faculty, or organization
- Send a join request or apply directly to a project team
- Built-in chat to discuss details before committing

## 3. Posting Events, Projects & Meetings

Student organizations (and individual students) can publish listings:

- Date, time, and location (on-campus or online)
- Type: event, ongoing project, recurring meeting, workshop, etc.
- Number of spots available
- Requirements (skills needed, prerequisites, commitment level)
- Format (one-time, weekly, semester-long)

## 4. Joining Others' Activities

Students browse a feed of upcoming events and projects:

- Send a request to join or simply RSVP
- Comment on details or ask questions
- Suggest changes, additional activities, or related ideas

## 6. Campus/Community Map

A visual map where students can:

- Mark buildings, clubs, or venues they've engaged with
- Leave notes about spaces (best study rooms, event venues, etc.)
- See what activities are happening where, and other students' check-ins

## 7. Notifications & Recommendations

Students get notified about:

- New events/projects matching their interests
- Requests to join their team or club
- Replies and comments on their posts

## 8. Integrations

For convenience, the platform can connect with:

- Google Calendar (personal scheduling)
- Corporate email auth

## Critical Analysis and Team Response

| Risk / Weakness | Explanation | Suggested Improvement | Team Summary |
|:----------------|:------------|:----------------------|:-------------|
| **R1 — Scope is unrealistic for a student project** | The first release includes profiles, search, events, team formation, applications, chat, comments, reviews, recommendations, notifications, a map, check-ins, calendar integration, and authentication. Each represents a substantial product area. Attempting all of them risks producing many incomplete features without validating the central idea. | Define an MVP around verified profiles, structured listings, search, and RSVP/join requests. Treat chat, reviews, recommendations, map/check-ins, and calendar integration as later phases. | The team agrees to narrow the MVP. Initially, student organizations will create events and opportunities, while students will discover them and later register. Student-led initiatives, advanced validation, chat, reviews, recommendations, map features, and integrations will be deferred to later phases. |
| **R2 — No primary user or initial use case is selected** | AlmaMater serves opportunity seekers, project initiators, organizations, and staff simultaneously. Their workflows and priorities differ, making product decisions and evaluation difficult. | Select one initial interaction, such as student organizations publishing opportunities for students to discover and join. Expand into peer team formation after establishing regular usage. | The initial focus will be student organizations as the main content publishers and students as the audience. The first use case is for organizations to publish events and opportunities that students can discover. |
| **R3 — The platform depends on content before it has users** | Students receive little value without current listings, while organizers receive little value without an active audience. Empty or outdated listings could quickly undermine trust. | Establish a launch strategy with several university organizations or departments committed to publishing and maintaining opportunities before student onboarding. | Before launch, the team plans to involve several key student organizations, seed the platform with events, and ask those organizations to promote it among students. |
| **R4 — Verification is treated as trust** | A corporate email confirms university affiliation but does not establish that a listing is official, a claimed skill is genuine, or a user will behave appropriately. Allowing every authenticated user to publish and contact others creates spam and impersonation risks. | Distinguish verified university membership from verified organization/staff status. Define reporting, moderation, content ownership, and organizer verification rules. | Corporate email will verify university membership, while content will also undergo automated checks. An administrator or moderator will handle complex cases and remove inappropriate listings. |
| **R5 — Privacy and safety boundaries are undefined** | Searchable skills, faculty details, direct invitations, reviews, map notes, and check-ins expose personal or location-related information. The README does not specify visibility controls or whether check-ins are live. | Make sensitive profile fields optional, provide visibility and contact controls, and avoid public live check-ins. Define blocking, reporting, and data-retention expectations. | Only a user's full name, faculty, major, and year of study will be required; all other profile information will be optional. Location tracking is not planned, so the team considers this risk limited. |
| **R6 — Reviews and endorsements may be unreliable or harmful** | The purpose and eligibility rules for reviews are unclear. Unverified endorsements can become popularity signals, while negative reviews may create disputes or reputational harm. | Remove reviews from the first release or restrict them to confirmed collaborations using structured, private feedback. Clearly define moderation and appeal rules before introducing public reputation features. | Reviews and participant endorsements will be removed from the MVP to avoid disputed ratings and additional moderation requirements. |
| **R7 — Joining workflows are inconsistent** | “RSVP,” “join request,” “apply,” and “one-click registration” appear across the README without defining when each applies or who approves participation. Events, selective projects, and recurring groups require different rules. | Define explicit listing modes: open RSVP, approval-required application, and external registration. Specify capacity, waitlist, cancellation, and acceptance behavior for each. | In the MVP, the organizer will choose how participants join: through an external Google Form, by contacting the project leader, or through open attendance without registration. A custom application workflow will not be developed yet. |
| **R8 — Personalization assumptions are unsupported** | Recommendations promise relevant opportunities, but a new single-university platform may initially have too little content and interaction data for meaningful personalization. | Begin with user-selected interests, filters, saved searches, or category subscriptions. Describe algorithmic recommendations as a later possibility rather than an initial value proposition. | Personalization will be postponed until the platform has enough users and data. Faculty and topic filters will be sufficient for the MVP, and algorithmic recommendations may not be necessary at all. |
| **R9 — Success is not defined** | The concept lists features and benefits but does not state what result would demonstrate that AlmaMater works. Registrations alone would not prove that students discover useful opportunities or form effective teams. | Define measurable outcomes such as active listings, listing-to-join conversion, successful team matches, repeat organizer usage, and participant attendance. | The team proposes measuring MVP success through the number of onboarded student organizations, system and integration stability, unique student-event interactions, and repeat use of the service. |
| **R10 — Adoption over existing tools is assumed** | Organizers already use Instagram, Telegram, Google Forms, email, and spreadsheets. AlmaMater adds value only if maintaining another channel is easier or produces meaningfully better reach. | Define a clear adoption advantage and minimize duplicate work—for example, reusable listings, centralized applications, exportable participant lists, or links that organizers can share through existing channels. | The main advantage is centralized event information and the ability to enter announcement details once and distribute them through Instagram, WhatsApp, and Outlook. The form or parser should remain simple to avoid expanding the MVP unnecessarily. |
